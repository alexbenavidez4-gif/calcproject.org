How to Measure a Room for Square Footage (Even If It's Not a Rectangle)

← All Guides

Almost every home improvement estimate starts with the same number: the square footage of a room. If that number is wrong, everything built on top of it is wrong too. The good news is that measuring a room takes only a tape measure, a notepad, and about ten minutes. This guide shows you how to get an accurate number for any room.

What You Need

  • A tape measure, ideally 25 feet or longer, or a laser measure.
  • A notepad or a phone to record the numbers.
  • A helper, if the room is long. One person can measure alone, but a second person keeps the tape straight.

The Basic Rectangular Room

Measure the length of the room along one wall, from corner to corner. Then measure the width along the adjacent wall. Multiply the two. A room that is 12 feet by 10 feet has an area of 12 x 10 = 120 square feet.

Walls are often not perfectly parallel, so measure each dimension in two or three places, and use the largest numbers for ordering material. A floor that comes up short by a few inches at one wall is a bigger problem than having a little extra.

L-Shaped and Odd Rooms

If the room is not a simple rectangle, split it into rectangles. Measure each one separately and add the areas together. For example, an L-shaped room might be a main space that is 12 feet by 10 feet, plus an alcove that is 6 feet by 8 feet. The areas are 120 and 48 square feet, so the total is 168 square feet.

For a bay window or a nook, treat it as a separate rectangle, or as a triangle if it has an angled wall. The area of a triangle is half the base times the height.

What to Include and What to Skip

  • Include closets and alcoves that will get the same flooring.
  • Skip areas covered by permanent cabinets, kitchen islands, and built-ins, since flooring is not installed underneath them. However, if you may remodel later, include them.
  • Include doorways where the flooring runs through.

Measuring Walls for Paint, Wallpaper, or Drywall

For wall jobs, you need the wall area, not the floor area. Add up all of the wall lengths, which is the perimeter, and multiply by the ceiling height. A 12-by-10 room has a perimeter of 44 feet, and with 8-foot ceilings that gives 44 x 8 = 352 square feet. Then subtract the openings. A standard door is about 21 square feet, and a typical window about 15 square feet. This is the same method the paint calculator uses.

Converting Units

  • Square feet to square yards: divide by 9. Carpet is often sold by the square yard.
  • Square feet to square meters: multiply by 0.0929.
  • Square meters to square feet: multiply by 10.764.

The calculators on CalcProject have a unit switch in the corner, so you can work in imperial or metric without converting by hand.

Common Measuring Mistakes

  • Measuring from the baseboard instead of the wall. Measure from the wall surface or from baseboard to baseboard consistently, and note which one you used.
  • Rounding down. Always round up when you are buying material.
  • Forgetting to write it down. Draw a quick sketch of the room, label each wall, and note every measurement, so you can check it later.
  • Mixing feet and inches. A measurement of 11 feet 6 inches is 11.5 feet, not 11.6. Convert inches by dividing by 12.

A Worked Example

You have a bedroom that measures 13 feet 6 inches by 11 feet 3 inches. Convert to decimal feet: 13.5 and 11.25. Multiply: 13.5 x 11.25 = 151.9 square feet. Add 10 percent for waste if you are buying flooring: about 167 square feet. If one box of flooring covers 20 square feet, divide 167 by 20 and round up to 9 boxes.

Tip: take a photo of your sketch and measurements with your phone, so you have it with you at the store.
