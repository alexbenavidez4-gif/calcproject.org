How to use the paint calculator

  1. Measure the length and width of the room and the wall height.
  2. Count the doors and windows. The calculator subtracts about 21 sq ft per door and 15 sq ft per window.
  3. Set the number of coats. Two coats is standard for a color change.
  4. Check the coverage on your paint can and change it if it is not 350 sq ft per gallon.

The formula

Wall area = (2 × length + 2 × width) × height, minus door and window area, × number of coats, ÷ coverage.

Worked example

For example, a 12×10 ft room with 8 ft walls, one door, two windows, and two coats needs roughly 2 gallons at 350 sq ft/gallon coverage.

Good to know

  • Fresh drywall, rough texture, and dark-to-light color changes soak up more paint. Drop the coverage to 250 to 300 sq ft per gallon.
  • Ceilings are not included. For a ceiling, multiply room length by width and divide by the coverage.
  • Buy all the paint for one room at once so the color batch matches.

Common questions

How much paint do I need for a 12x12 room?

A 12 × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls has about 384 sq ft of wall. After one door and two windows, two coats take roughly 2 gallons at 350 sq ft per gallon.

How many square feet does a gallon of paint cover?

Most interior paint covers 350 to 400 square feet per gallon on smooth, primed walls. Primer covers less, about 200 to 300 square feet.

Do I need two coats of paint?

Usually yes. One coat can work when you repaint the same color with a quality paint, but a color change almost always needs two.
