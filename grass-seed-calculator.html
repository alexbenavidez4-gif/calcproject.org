How to use the grass seed calculator

  1. Measure the lawn length and width in feet.
  2. Find the new-lawn seeding rate on the seed bag and enter it.
  3. Choose new lawn or overseeding.

The formula

Pounds of seed = lawn area (sq ft) ÷ 1,000 × seeding rate (lb per 1,000 sq ft). Overseeding uses about half the new-lawn rate.

Worked example

A 40 × 30 ft lawn is 1,200 sq ft. At 7 lb per 1,000 sq ft, a new lawn needs 8.4 lb of seed. Overseeding the same lawn needs 4.2 lb.

Good to know

  • Typical new-lawn rates per 1,000 sq ft: tall fescue 6 to 8 lb, perennial ryegrass 6 to 8 lb, Kentucky bluegrass 2 to 3 lb, bermuda 1 to 2 lb.
  • More seed is not better. Crowded seedlings compete and thin out.
  • Split the seed in half and spread it in two passes at right angles for even coverage.

Common questions

How much grass seed do I need per 1,000 square feet?

It depends on the grass. Tall fescue and ryegrass need 6 to 8 lb, Kentucky bluegrass 2 to 3 lb, and bermuda 1 to 2 lb for a new lawn.

How much seed do I need for overseeding?

About half the new-lawn rate. For tall fescue that is 3 to 4 lb per 1,000 square feet.

When is the best time to plant grass seed?

Early fall for cool-season grasses like fescue and bluegrass. Late spring for warm-season grasses like bermuda and zoysia.
