How it works, examples and FAQ

Percent of a number: divide the percent by 100, then multiply. 20% of 150 is 0.20 × 150 = 30.

What percent is it? divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100. 30 out of 120 is 30 ÷ 120 = 0.25, which is 25%.

Percent change: subtract old from new, divide by the old value, multiply by 100. Going from 50 to 65 is (65 − 50) ÷ 50 = 0.30, a 30% increase.

How do I calculate a discount?
Use the first tab. A 25% discount on a $80 jacket: 25% of 80 = 20, so you save $20 and pay $60.

Why can't the old value be zero in percent change?
Percent change measures growth relative to where you started. Starting from zero, any growth is infinite in relative terms, so the math has no answer.

Can the answer be over 100%?
Yes. If the part is bigger than the whole, or a value more than doubles, the percent is over 100.
