How to use the baseboard & trim calculator

  1. Measure the room length and width.
  2. Count the doorways and enter their average width. Baseboard stops at door casings.
  3. Set a waste allowance. Ten percent covers miter cuts in a simple room.
  4. Enter the board length your store stocks: 8, 12, or 16 ft.

The formula

Perimeter minus doorway widths, plus waste, divided by board length.

Worked example

For example, a 12×10 ft room with one 3 ft doorway needs about 6 boards at 8 ft each.

Good to know

  • For crown molding, set doorways to 0, because crown runs across the top of every wall.
  • Longer boards mean fewer joints. Use one full board per wall where you can.
  • Rooms with many inside and outside corners need closer to 15 percent waste.

Common questions

How do I calculate linear feet of baseboard?

Add the length of every wall, then subtract the width of each doorway. A 12 × 10 ft room has a 44 ft perimeter; with one 3 ft doorway you need 41 linear feet before waste.

How much extra baseboard should I buy?

Buy about 10 percent extra for a simple room and 15 percent for a room with many corners or if you are new to cutting miters.

What lengths does baseboard come in?

Common lengths are 8, 12, and 16 feet. Primed MDF and finger-jointed pine are often sold in 16 ft lengths.
