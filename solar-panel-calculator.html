How to use this calculator

  1. Fill in Monthly electric use, Peak sun hours per day, Panel size and System efficiency.

The formula

System size (kW) = monthly kWh ÷ 30 ÷ peak sun hours ÷ system efficiency. Panels = system watts ÷ panel watts.

Worked example

With the starting numbers on this page (Monthly electric use: 900; Peak sun hours per day: 5; Panel size: 400; System efficiency: 80), the result is Panels 19. It also shows Daily use: 30.0 kWh; System size: 7.50 kW.

Good to know

  • Peak sun hours run from about 3 in the Pacific Northwest to 6 or more in the Southwest. Efficiency covers inverter, wiring, and dirt losses.
  • A typical home panel takes up 18 to 21 square feet of roof.
  • Your electric bill lists your kWh use. Average the last 12 months to smooth out summer and winter.

Common questions

How many solar panels do I need?

Divide your daily kWh by your peak sun hours and by 0.8 for losses to get the system size. At 900 kWh a month and 5 sun hours, that is a 7.5 kW system, or 19 panels of 400 watts.

What are peak sun hours?

The number of hours a day when sunlight averages 1,000 watts per square meter. It is not the same as daylight hours.

How much roof space do solar panels need?

Nineteen panels need roughly 340 to 400 square feet of unshaded roof, ideally facing south.
