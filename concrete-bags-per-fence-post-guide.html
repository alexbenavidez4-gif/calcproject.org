How Many Bags of Concrete Per Fence Post? A Chart by Post and Hole Size

← All Guides

Quick answer: a standard 4x4 fence post in a 10-inch wide, 30-inch deep hole takes about 1.15 cubic feet of concrete. That is roughly two 80 lb bags, three 60 lb bags, or four 50 lb bags. The exact number depends on the post size, the width and depth of the hole, and the bag size, so the charts below cover the common combinations.

How the Number Is Worked Out

The concrete fills the hole around the post, so the amount is the volume of the hole minus the volume of the post.

  1. Hole volume = 3.1416 × (width ÷ 2)² × depth. For a 10-inch wide, 30-inch deep hole: 3.1416 × 5² × 30 = 2,356 cubic inches.
  2. Post volume = post width × post width × depth. A “4x4” is really 3.5 inches square: 3.5 × 3.5 × 30 = 368 cubic inches.
  3. Concrete needed = 2,356 − 368 = 1,988 cubic inches, which is 1,988 ÷ 1,728 = 1.15 cubic feet.
  4. Bags = cubic feet ÷ bag yield. An 80 lb bag yields about 0.60 cubic feet, a 60 lb bag about 0.45, and a 50 lb bag about 0.375. So 1.15 ÷ 0.60 = 1.9 bags of 80 lb.

Buy 5 to 10 percent extra. Holes are never perfectly shaped, and it is cheaper to carry back an unopened bag than to make a second trip with a half-set post.

Picking the Hole Size

  • Width: about three times the width of the post is a common rule. Wider holes grip better but cost more in concrete.
  • Depth: at least one third of the post’s length in the ground, and below the frost line in cold climates. See fence post depth and spacing.
  • Bag size: 50 lb bags are easier to carry. 80 lb bags are usually cheaper per cubic foot.
Before you dig: call 811 in the U.S. for a free utility locate. Hitting a buried line is a real risk with any post hole.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many bags of concrete do I need for one fence post?
For a standard 4x4 post in a 10-inch wide, 30-inch deep hole, plan on about 1.9 bags of 80 lb concrete, 2.6 bags of 60 lb, or 3.1 bags of 50 lb. Round up, so that is 2, 3 or 4 bags.

How many bags of concrete for a mailbox post?
A mailbox post is usually a 4x4 set about 24 inches deep. Use the 24-inch rows of the 4x4 chart: about 1 to 3 bags of 80 lb concrete (0.9 bags in an 8-inch hole, 1.5 in a 10-inch hole, 2.3 in a 12-inch hole).

How wide should a fence post hole be?
A common rule is a hole about three times as wide as the post, so about 10 to 12 inches for a 4x4. Wider holes use a lot more concrete: going from 10 to 12 inches wide adds about 50 percent.

How deep should the hole be?
At least one third of the total post length should be in the ground, and deeper than your local frost line in cold climates. A 6-foot fence post is usually set about 2 feet deep, and taller or heavier fences go deeper.

Does the chart include gravel at the bottom of the hole?
No. The chart assumes concrete fills the whole hole around the post. If you put 4 to 6 inches of gravel under the post for drainage, you need slightly less concrete, so the chart leaves you a small safety margin.

Can I pour dry concrete into the hole?
Some fast-setting mixes are made to be poured in dry and then watered, but others must be mixed first. Follow the directions on your bag.
