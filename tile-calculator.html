How to use the tile calculator

  1. Measure the length and width of the area to tile.
  2. Enter the tile length and width in inches.
  3. Set the waste allowance: 10 percent for a straight grid, 15 percent for diagonal or large-format tile.
  4. Divide the tile count by the tiles per box to get boxes.

The formula

Area = length × width, plus waste, divided by the square footage of one tile.

Worked example

For example, a 10×8 ft floor with 12×12 in tiles and 10% waste needs about 88 tiles.

Good to know

  • For a backsplash, enter the counter run as the length and the height in feet as the width. Eighteen inches is 1.5 ft.
  • Large tiles in small rooms waste more because every edge tile is cut.
  • Keep a few spare tiles. Matching a discontinued tile later is nearly impossible.

Common questions

How many 12x12 tiles do I need for 100 square feet?

Each 12 × 12 inch tile covers 1 square foot, so 100 tiles plus 10 percent waste: 110 tiles.

How much extra tile should I buy?

Ten percent for straight layouts, 15 percent for diagonal patterns or rooms with many cuts, and up to 20 percent for herringbone.

How do I calculate tiles per square foot?

Multiply the tile length by its width in inches and divide 144 by the result. A 6 × 24 inch plank covers 1 square foot, so you need 1 per square foot.
