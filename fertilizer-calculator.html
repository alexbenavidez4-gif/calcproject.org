The formula

Pounds of product = lawn area ÷ 1,000 × nitrogen rate ÷ (nitrogen percent ÷ 100).

Worked example

With the starting numbers on this page (Lawn area: 5000 sq ft; Nitrogen rate: 1; Nitrogen on the bag: 24; Bag weight: 40), the result is Fertilizer needed 20.8 lb. It also shows Product per 1,000 sq ft: 4.2 lb; Bags: 1 bag.

Good to know

  • The three numbers on a bag are nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium. Most lawns take 0.5 to 1 lb of nitrogen per 1,000 sq ft per feeding. The area and rates here are entered in imperial units.
  • Do not apply more than 1 lb of nitrogen per 1,000 square feet in one feeding. More than that can burn the lawn.
  • Water lightly after spreading granular fertilizer unless the bag says not to.

Common questions

How do I read the numbers on a fertilizer bag?

The three numbers are the percent of nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium by weight. A 40 lb bag of 24-0-6 holds 9.6 lb of nitrogen.

How much fertilizer do I need for 5,000 square feet?

For 1 lb of nitrogen per 1,000 square feet with a 24 percent nitrogen product, you need about 20.8 lb.

How often should I fertilize my lawn?

Two to four times a year during the growing season for most lawns. Warm-season grasses are fed from late spring through summer, and cool-season grasses mainly in fall.
