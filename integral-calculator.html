How it works, examples and FAQ

The reverse power rule says the integral of c·x^n is c·x^(n+1) ÷ (n+1), plus a constant C. Raise the power by one, then divide by the new power.

Example: ∫ (3x^2 + 2x) dx = x^3 + x^2 + C.

For a definite integral, compute F(upper) − F(lower). From 0 to 2: F(2) = 8 + 4 = 12 and F(0) = 0, so the answer is 12. This is the area under the curve.

Heads up: this version handles polynomials. More function types are on the roadmap.

Why is there a + C?
Many functions share the same derivative; they differ only by a constant. C stands for that unknown constant.

Does the definite integral need + C?
No. It cancels when you subtract.

Can it integrate 1/x or sin(x)?
Not yet. This first version covers polynomials.
