How it works, examples and FAQ

Past years: the calculator uses the official Consumer Price Index (CPI-U) from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. It divides the index for the later year by the index for the earlier year, then multiplies your amount by that ratio. $100 in 2000 buys what $186.96 buys in 2025, because the index went from 172.2 to 321.943.

Future cost: pick a yearly inflation rate and a number of years. Prices grow by (1 + rate) every year, so something that costs $1,000 today costs $1,343.92 in 10 years at 3% a year, and $1,000 will only buy $744.09 worth of today's goods.

For scale, $1 in 1913 buys what $32.52 buys in 2025.

Data: annual average CPI-U, U.S. city average, all items, 1982-84 = 100, from 1913 to 2025. The 2025 figure is the average of the 11 months published (October 2025 was not collected during the federal funding lapse).

What is the CPI?
The Consumer Price Index tracks the average price of a basket of everyday goods and services. When the index rises, the same basket costs more, which is inflation.

Is this the official inflation rate?
It uses the official CPI-U series for all urban consumers. Your own inflation can be higher or lower, since a rise in rent or gas affects people differently.

Why does money lose value over time?
When prices rise, each dollar buys less. A dollar in 1913 bought far more than a dollar buys today, so older amounts have to be scaled up to compare them fairly.

Can I go backwards in time?
Yes. Put the later year in the first box and the earlier year in the second. The result then shows what today's money would have been worth back then.

What if I need this year or next year?
Use the second tab. Enter an expected yearly inflation rate and a number of years to project future prices.
