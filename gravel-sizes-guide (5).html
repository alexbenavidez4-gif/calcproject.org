Gravel Sizes Explained: Which Size to Use for Driveways, Paths, and Drainage

← All Guides

Walk into a landscape supply yard and you will meet a list of names that mean nothing to a beginner: pea gravel, number 57, number 67, crusher run, river rock. They are not just brand names. Each one is a different size or type of stone, and choosing the wrong one gives you a driveway that rolls under your tires or a path that is painful to walk on. This guide explains the common types and where each one belongs.

How Gravel Is Sized

Crushed stone is sorted by passing it through screens. The numbers, such as #57 or #67, are standard grading designations that describe the range of stone sizes in the pile. Smaller numbers in the middle of the range usually mean larger stones, but the details vary, and suppliers in different regions use different names for similar products. When you call, tell the supplier what you are building and ask which product they recommend. They know what is local and what works.

The Common Types

  • Pea gravel: small, rounded stones about 3/8 inch across. Comfortable underfoot, so it works for paths, play areas, and around patios. Because the stones are round, they shift and do not lock together, so pea gravel is a poor choice for a driveway.
  • #57 stone: clean, angular crushed stone with pieces about 3/4 to 1 inch. A very common choice for drainage, French drains, and the base layer under pavers or slabs. The angular edges lock together and the gaps between the stones let water pass.
  • #67 stone: similar to #57 but a little smaller, around 3/4 inch. Often used for driveways and as a general-purpose gravel.
  • Crusher run (also called quarry process or road base): a mix of crushed stone and fine stone dust. It compacts very hard, making it the usual choice for a driveway base or under a patio. It does not drain well, so do not use it where you need water to flow through.
  • River rock: smooth, rounded stones, often 1 to 3 inches or bigger. Mostly decorative, used in garden beds, along dry creek beds, and around downspouts.

Which Gravel for Which Job?

  • Driveway: a base of crusher run, topped with #67 or similar angular stone.
  • Walking path: pea gravel or fine crushed stone, over a compacted base.
  • Patio or paver base: crusher run or a similar compactable mix, tamped in layers.
  • Drainage trench or behind a retaining wall: clean #57 stone with no fines.
  • Decorative beds: river rock or any stone you like the look of.

How Deep Should It Be?

Paths and decorative areas usually need 2 to 3 inches. Driveways need a lot more, commonly 4 to 6 inches or more in total, spread and compacted in layers. A weed barrier fabric under the gravel helps keep the stone from sinking into soft soil and slows weeds. For a driveway on soft ground, a layer of geotextile fabric under the base makes it last much longer.

Converting to Tons and Yards

Gravel is sold by the cubic yard or by the ton. A cubic yard of crushed stone typically weighs around 1.3 to 1.5 tons, depending on the stone and its moisture. At a 2-inch depth, one cubic yard covers about 160 square feet, and one ton covers roughly 100 to 120 square feet. Ask your supplier for the weight per yard for the exact product, since that number is what turns your calculator result into a purchase.

Gravel also settles and compacts after it is placed, so order about 5 to 10 percent extra to make up for what you lose to settling.

Quick rule: round stones for walking and looks, angular stones for driving and drainage, and crusher run for a solid base that you want to compact.
