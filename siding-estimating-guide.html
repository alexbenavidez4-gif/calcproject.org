How to Estimate Siding: Squares, Gables, Windows, and Waste

← All Guides

Siding is one of the more expensive materials on a house, and ordering too little or too much costs real money. The calculation is not hard, but most people forget one or two pieces, usually the triangular gable ends or the trim. This guide walks through the full estimate from start to finish.

Siding Is Sold in Squares

Like roofing, siding is priced and packaged by the square, which is 100 square feet of wall. Vinyl siding is typically sold in boxes that cover a set number of square feet, and fiber cement and wood siding are sold by the piece. In every case, you begin with the total wall area.

Step 1: Measure the Walls

For each wall, multiply the width by the height. For a simple rectangular house, you can add up the perimeter and multiply by the wall height. A house 40 feet by 30 feet has a perimeter of 140 feet. At a wall height of 9 feet, that is 140 x 9 = 1,260 square feet.

If the house has two stories, measure each story separately. Remember to measure the exterior wall height from the bottom of the siding, which is often near the foundation, up to the soffit.

Step 2: Add the Gables

The triangle of wall at the end of a gabled roof is easy to forget and is a large area. The area of a triangle is half its base times its height. A gable that is 30 feet wide and 8 feet tall from the base to the peak is 30 x 8 / 2 = 120 square feet. A house with two gable ends has 240 square feet of gable.

Step 3: Subtract Windows and Doors

Windows and doors do not need siding, so subtract them. A typical window is around 15 square feet and a standard door about 21 square feet, though your windows may be larger. Measure the real sizes if you can. Large garage doors and picture windows can be significant. Some installers subtract only large openings and leave small ones in the total as extra waste, so decide on a method and stay with it.

Worked Example

  1. Walls: 1,260 square feet.
  2. Two gables: 240 square feet. Running total: 1,500 square feet.
  3. Ten windows at 15 square feet and two doors at 21 square feet: 150 + 42 = 192 square feet of openings.
  4. Net area: 1,500 - 192 = 1,308 square feet.
  5. Add 10 percent for waste: 1,308 x 1.10 = about 1,439 square feet.
  6. In squares: 14.4, so order 15 squares.

How Much Waste?

Ten percent is a good starting point for a simple house. Add more, perhaps 12 to 15 percent, for houses with lots of corners, angles, dormers, or a diagonal pattern. Siding is cut at every window, door, and corner, and the leftover pieces are often too short to use.

Don't Forget the Accessories

The panels are only part of the order. A complete siding job also needs:

  • Corner posts for each inside and outside corner.
  • J-channel around windows, doors, and under the eaves.
  • Starter strip along the bottom edge of the first row.
  • Trim, flashing, and house wrap if the old layers are removed.
  • The correct nails or fasteners. Vinyl siding needs nails that are not driven tight, because the panels must move as temperatures change.

Order for Consistency

As with flooring and wallpaper, color can vary slightly between production batches. Order all of your siding together, and keep a few spare panels in case of future damage. Siding exposed to sun for years can fade, so a replacement panel from a new batch may not match a ten-year-old wall perfectly.

Quick method: add the walls and gables, subtract openings, add 10 percent, divide by 100, and round up. Add accessories separately.
