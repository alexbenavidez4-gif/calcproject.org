How it works, examples and FAQ

To solve ax + b = c you undo the operations in reverse order. First move b to the other side by subtracting it from both sides. Then divide both sides by a.

Example: 2x + 3 = 11. Subtract 3: 2x = 8. Divide by 2: x = 4. Check: 2 × 4 + 3 = 11. ✓

Whatever you do to one side of an equation, you must do to the other. That is the one rule behind all of algebra.

What if my equation has x on both sides?
Move the x terms to one side first. For 5x + 2 = 2x + 11, subtract 2x and 2 from both sides to get 3x = 9, then enter a = 3, b = 0, c = 9.

Why can't a be 0?
Then the x disappears and you do not have an equation to solve for x.
