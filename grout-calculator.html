How to use the grout calculator

  1. Enter the tile length, width, and thickness in inches.
  2. Enter the joint width you will use. Spacers are marked with their size.
  3. Enter the tiled area in square feet and pick the bag size.

The formula

Joint volume per square foot = 144 × (tile length + tile width) ÷ (tile length × tile width) × joint width × tile thickness, in cubic inches. That is multiplied by about 0.058 lb per cubic inch for cement grout, then by your area and waste.

Worked example

For 12 × 12 inch tile, 3/8 inch thick, with 1/8 inch joints, grout runs about 0.065 lb per square foot. Over 100 sq ft with 10 percent waste that is about 7.2 lb, so one 25 lb bag is plenty.

Good to know

  • Small tile and mosaics use far more grout than large tile because there are more joints per square foot.
  • Use unsanded grout for joints narrower than 1/8 inch and sanded grout for wider joints.
  • Coverage varies by brand. Check the chart on the bag before you buy, and get one extra bag from the same color lot.

Common questions

How much grout do I need for 100 square feet?

It depends on the tile. Large 12 × 12 inch tile with 1/8 inch joints needs about 7 lb. Two inch mosaics with the same joint need roughly 25 to 30 lb.

How many square feet does a 25 lb bag of grout cover?

Anywhere from about 50 square feet with small mosaic tile to more than 300 square feet with large tile and narrow joints.

Should I use sanded or unsanded grout?

Sanded grout is for joints 1/8 inch and wider. Unsanded grout is for narrower joints and for polished stone or glass that sand could scratch.
