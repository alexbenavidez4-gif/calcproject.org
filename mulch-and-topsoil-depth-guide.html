Mulch and Topsoil Depth: How Many Cubic Yards or Bags You Really Need

← All Guides

Mulch and soil are sold by volume, but you measure a garden bed by area. The missing piece is depth, and a small change in depth makes a big change in how much you buy. This guide shows how to choose the right depth and convert it into cubic yards or bags.

How Deep Should Mulch Be?

For most garden beds, 2 to 3 inches of mulch is the right depth. Less than 2 inches does not block weeds or hold moisture well. More than 3 or 4 inches can keep water and air from reaching the roots and can encourage rot and pests. Fine mulches should be used at the lower end of the range, since they pack down tighter than chunky bark.

Around trees, spread mulch in a wide ring, but pull it back from the trunk so there is a small gap of bare soil around the base. A pile of mulch touching the trunk, sometimes called a mulch volcano, holds moisture against the bark and can damage the tree.

How Deep for Topsoil?

  • New lawn: roughly 4 to 6 inches of good topsoil is a common target for seed or sod over poor soil.
  • Raised beds and vegetable gardens: 6 to 12 inches, depending on what you grow.
  • Leveling low spots: only as much as you need, but add it in thin layers.

The Math: Area, Depth, Volume

Volume is area times depth. The catch is the units. Depth is in inches, and bulk material is sold in cubic yards, which is 27 cubic feet. To convert, change the depth to feet by dividing by 12, multiply by the area in square feet to get cubic feet, and divide by 27 for cubic yards.

A useful shortcut: one cubic yard covers about 324 square feet at a depth of 1 inch, about 162 square feet at 2 inches, about 108 square feet at 3 inches, and about 81 square feet at 4 inches.

Worked Example

A garden bed is 200 square feet and you want 3 inches of mulch.

  1. Depth in feet: 3 / 12 = 0.25 feet.
  2. Volume: 200 x 0.25 = 50 cubic feet.
  3. Cubic yards: 50 / 27 = 1.85 yards, so order 2 yards for bulk delivery.
  4. In bags: a 2-cubic-foot bag holds 2 cubic feet, so you need 50 / 2 = 25 bags.

Bulk or Bags?

Bagged mulch is easy to carry, and good for small jobs and tight access. For anything over about 2 or 3 cubic yards, bulk delivery is usually cheaper per cubic foot, since a cubic yard equals around 13 bags of 2 cubic feet each. The tradeoff is that bulk arrives as a pile in your driveway, and you must move it with a wheelbarrow, so plan the space and the labor. Many suppliers have a minimum order or a delivery fee, so compare the total cost.

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting depth when comparing prices. A bag labeled 2 cubic feet at a low price might hold less than a competitor's.
  • Piling mulch on top of old mulch. Old mulch breaks down. Rake it, and add only a thin top-up layer, so the total depth stays under 3 inches.
  • Skipping edging. Without a border, mulch and soil drift onto the lawn.
  • Using landscape fabric under everything. It can stop weeds for a time, but it also blocks organic matter from reaching the soil, and weeds eventually grow in the mulch on top.
Quick method: area x depth in feet = cubic feet. Divide by 27 for yards, or by the bag size for bags. Round up.
