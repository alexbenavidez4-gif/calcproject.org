How it works: add up the running watts of everything on at once, then add the biggest extra jump from a motor starting up (only one usually starts at a time). The suggested size adds about 20% headroom. Example: a fridge, sump pump, lights, TV, and Wi-Fi run about 2,100 W, and the fridge's startup needs about 1,500 W more, so a generator rated around 2,600 running and 4,400 starting watts fits.
⚠️ Watt values are typical. Check each appliance's label. Never plug a generator into a wall outlet. Use a transfer switch or interlock installed by a licensed electrician, and run generators outdoors, far from windows.

Common questions

What size generator do I need to run a refrigerator?

A refrigerator runs on about 700 watts but needs around 2,200 watts for a moment when the compressor starts. A 3,000 watt generator handles a fridge plus lights and chargers.

What is the difference between running watts and starting watts?

Running watts are what an appliance uses steadily. Starting watts are the short surge a motor needs to start, often two to three times the running watts.

Can I plug a generator into a wall outlet?

No. Backfeeding through an outlet can electrocute utility workers and start fires. Use a transfer switch or interlock installed by a licensed electrician.
