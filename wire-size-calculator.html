How it works: voltage drop = 2 × distance × wire resistance × amps. The suggested size is the smallest wire rated for your amps (using the common 60°C table values for home wiring) that also keeps the drop at 3% or less. Example: 20 amps at 120 V over 100 feet of 12 AWG copper drops about 7.7 volts (6.4%), so 8 AWG is the smallest size that stays under 3%.
⚠️ Estimates only. Wire size also depends on insulation type, temperature, conduit fill, and your local code. Have an electrician confirm before you buy.

Common questions

What size wire do I need for a 100 foot run?

It depends on the amps. For 20 amps at 120 V over 100 feet, 8 AWG copper keeps voltage drop under 3 percent. For 30 amps at 240 V over 100 feet, 8 AWG copper also works.

How much voltage drop is acceptable?

The National Electrical Code recommends, but does not require, no more than 3 percent drop on a branch circuit and 5 percent in total for feeder plus branch.

Does voltage drop depend on one-way or round-trip distance?

Current travels out and back, so the math uses twice the one-way distance. Enter the one-way distance here and the calculator doubles it.
