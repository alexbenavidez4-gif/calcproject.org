How to use the wall framing calculator

  1. Measure the wall length along the floor.
  2. Choose the stud spacing. Most walls are framed 16 inches on center.
  3. Count the corners or wall ends and the door and window openings.
  4. Buy plate lumber in the longest lengths you can carry to cut down on splices.

The formula

Studs = wall length (in) ÷ spacing, rounded up, plus 1 for the end. Add 2 studs for each corner or wall end and 2 for each door or window, then add waste. Plates = wall length × 3 (one bottom plate and a double top plate).

Worked example

A 20 ft wall at 16 inches on center needs 240 ÷ 16 + 1 = 16 studs. Two corners and one doorway add 6 more, for 22. With 10 percent waste, buy 25 studs and 60 linear feet of plate.

Good to know

  • Use pressure-treated lumber for any bottom plate that sits on concrete.
  • Headers, cripples, and sills for wide openings are extra. Add them from your plan.
  • A quick check used by framers: one stud per linear foot of wall covers studs, corners, and openings at 16 inches on center.

Common questions

How many studs do I need for a 10 foot wall?

At 16 inches on center, a 10 ft wall needs 120 ÷ 16 = 7.5, rounded up to 8, plus 1 for the end: 9 studs before corners and openings.

How far apart should wall studs be?

Sixteen inches on center is standard for most walls. Twenty-four inches on center is allowed for some non-load-bearing and 2x6 walls. Check your local code.

What does on center mean?

It means the distance from the center of one stud to the center of the next. It keeps the edges of 4 ft wide sheets landing on a stud.
