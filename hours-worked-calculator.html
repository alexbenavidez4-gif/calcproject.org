How it works, examples and FAQ

One shift: turn the start and end times into minutes after midnight, subtract, then take off the unpaid break. 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM is 510 minutes; with a 30-minute break that is 480 minutes, or exactly 8 hours.

Weekly timesheet: do the same for each day you worked and add the days together. Leave a day empty if you did not work. Five days of 7 hours 30 minutes is 37 hours 30 minutes, or 37.5 decimal hours.

Time formats you can type: 9, 9:00, 9:00 AM, 9am, 5:30 pm, 17:30 or 1730. Without AM or PM, the 24-hour clock is used.

Decimal hours: use the minutes to decimal calculator or divide minutes by 60. Payroll and invoices often need hours as decimals, so 7 hours 45 minutes is entered as 7.75.

How do I calculate hours worked?
Subtract the start time from the end time, then subtract any unpaid break. For example, 8:30 AM to 5:00 PM is 8 hours 30 minutes, and with a 30-minute unpaid lunch it is 8 hours.

How do I calculate hours worked with a lunch break?
Subtract the unpaid break from the total time between clocking in and out. If the break is paid, do not subtract it. Enter the minutes of unpaid break in the break box, or leave it empty for none.

How do I calculate hours worked on an overnight shift?
If the end time is earlier than the start time, the shift passes midnight, so the calculator adds 24 hours to the end time. For example, 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM is 8 hours.

How do I convert minutes to decimal hours?
Divide the minutes by 60. 15 minutes is 0.25 hours, 30 minutes is 0.5, 45 minutes is 0.75, and 7 hours 45 minutes is 7.75 hours. Payroll systems usually round to two decimals, so 20 minutes is 0.33 hours.

How do I add up a whole week of hours?
Choose the weekly timesheet. Enter a start time, end time, and break for each day you worked, and leave days off empty. The calculator adds every day and shows the weekly total in hours and minutes and in decimal hours.

How is overtime calculated?
Under U.S. federal rules, covered non-exempt employees must be paid at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Some states and employers also have daily overtime rules. The weekly timesheet can apply the 40-hour rule when you pick it, but check your own state and employer rules.

Is the pay shown before or after taxes?
Before taxes. The calculator multiplies your decimal hours by your hourly rate, rounded to the cent. Taxes, tips and other deductions are not included.
