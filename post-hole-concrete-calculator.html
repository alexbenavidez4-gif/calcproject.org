How to use the post hole concrete calculator

  1. Enter the hole diameter. Three times the post width is the usual rule: 10 to 12 inches for a 4x4.
  2. Enter the hole depth. Plan for one third of the post length in the ground, and below your frost line.
  3. Pick the post size, the number of posts, and the bag you plan to buy.

The formula

Concrete per hole = (3.1416 × hole radius² × depth) minus (post width² × depth), in cubic inches, divided by 1,728 to get cubic feet. Bags = total cubic feet ÷ bag yield, rounded up.

Worked example

A 10 inch wide, 30 inch deep hole around a 4x4 post takes about 1.15 cubic feet of concrete. Ten posts need 11.5 cubic feet, or 31 bags of 50 lb fast-setting mix.

Source: Quikrete Concrete Mix data sheet (bag yields)

Good to know

  • Put a few inches of gravel in the bottom of each hole for drainage before the post goes in.
  • Fast-setting mix can be poured dry into the hole and watered, with no mixing.
  • Slope the top of the concrete away from the post so water runs off.

Common questions

How many bags of concrete do I need per fence post?

A 4x4 post in a 10 inch wide, 30 inch deep hole takes about 3 bags of 50 lb fast-setting mix, or 2 bags of 80 lb mix.

How deep should a fence post hole be?

Bury at least one third of the post. A 6 ft tall fence on 8 ft posts needs holes about 24 to 30 inches deep, and deeper where the ground freezes.

How wide should a post hole be?

About three times the width of the post: 10 to 12 inches for a 4x4 and 16 to 18 inches for a 6x6.
