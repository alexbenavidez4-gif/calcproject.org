The formula

CFM = room volume (length × width × height) × air changes per hour ÷ 60.

Worked example

With the starting numbers on this page (Room length: 12 ft; Room width: 10 ft; Ceiling height: 8 ft; Air changes per hour: 8), the result is Airflow needed 128 CFM. It also shows Room volume: 960 cu ft.

Good to know

  • Bathrooms need about 8 air changes per hour, kitchens 15, and workshops 6 to 10. A simpler bathroom rule is 1 CFM per square foot.
  • Building codes generally require at least a 50 CFM fan in a bathroom.
  • Long or kinked ducts cut airflow. Go up a fan size if the duct run is over about 10 ft.

Common questions

How many CFM do I need for a bathroom fan?

One CFM per square foot of floor is the usual rule, with a 50 CFM minimum. An 80 square foot bathroom needs an 80 CFM fan.

How do I calculate CFM for a room?

Multiply the room volume in cubic feet by the air changes per hour you want and divide by 60.

What are air changes per hour?

How many times an hour the fan replaces all the air in the room. Bathrooms need about 8, kitchens about 15, and workshops 6 to 10.
