How to use the concrete block calculator

  1. Measure the wall length and height in feet.
  2. Leave the block size at 16 × 8 inches for standard block, or change it for half-high or other sizes.
  3. Subtract large door or window openings from the wall length if you want a tighter count.

The formula

Blocks = wall area (length × height) ÷ the face area of one block, plus waste. A standard block is 16 × 8 inches with its mortar joint, which is 0.89 sq ft, or 1.125 blocks per square foot. Mortar is estimated at one 80 lb bag of premixed mortar for every 12 blocks.

Worked example

A 20 × 8 ft wall is 160 sq ft, which takes 180 standard blocks. With 5 percent waste, buy 189 blocks and about 16 bags of mortar mix.

Good to know

  • Block sizes are nominal. A block sold as 8x8x16 really measures 7 5/8 × 7 5/8 × 15 5/8 inches, and the 3/8 inch joint makes up the difference.
  • Block walls need a concrete footing, usually twice as wide as the wall.
  • Mortar use varies with joint thickness and how much you drop. Check the yield printed on the bag.

Common questions

How many concrete blocks do I need per square foot?

For standard 8x8x16 block, 1.125 blocks per square foot of wall, or 112.5 blocks per 100 square feet.

How many blocks are in a pallet?

A pallet of standard 8x8x16 block usually holds 72 to 90 blocks, depending on the supplier.

Do I need to fill the blocks with concrete?

Structural and retaining walls usually need rebar and grout in some or all cores. Check local code and an engineer for any wall that holds back soil or supports a roof.
