How to use the AC Size (BTU) calculator

  1. Measure the room length and width in feet.
  2. Enter the ceiling height and how many people normally use the room.
  3. Pick the sun exposure and say whether the room is a kitchen.
  4. Buy the closest size at or just above the result.

The formula

Start with the ENERGY STAR capacity for the room area. Increase it 10 percent for a very sunny room or reduce it 10 percent for heavy shade. Add 600 BTU for each person beyond two, and 4,000 BTU for a kitchen. The chart assumes 8 ft ceilings, so taller rooms are scaled up in proportion.

Worked example

A 15 × 12 ft bedroom is 180 sq ft. The chart calls for 6,000 BTU per hour for 150 to 250 sq ft, and with average sun and two people no adjustment is needed.

Source: ENERGY STAR: room air conditioner sizing chart

Good to know

  • Bigger is not better. An oversized unit cools fast, shuts off before it removes humidity, and leaves the room clammy.
  • Open floor plans count as one room. Include every connected space with no door.
  • This chart is for room air conditioners. Whole-house central systems need a contractor load calculation (Manual J).

Common questions

How many BTUs do I need per square foot?

Roughly 20 to 25 BTU per square foot for small rooms. The ENERGY STAR chart is more exact: 5,000 BTU for 100 to 150 square feet, 8,000 for 300 to 350, 12,000 for 450 to 550.

What size air conditioner do I need for a 12x12 room?

A 12 × 12 ft room is 144 square feet, which calls for a 5,000 BTU unit under normal conditions.

How many BTUs are in a ton of cooling?

One ton of cooling is 12,000 BTU per hour. A 24,000 BTU unit is a 2 ton unit.
