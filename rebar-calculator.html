How to use the rebar calculator

  1. Measure the slab length and width in feet.
  2. Choose the grid spacing. Twelve to 18 inches is common for slabs and driveways.
  3. Enter the bar length your supplier sells. Twenty feet is standard.

The formula

Bars each way = (slab side minus 3 inches of edge clearance on both ends) ÷ spacing, rounded down, plus 1. Total length = bars × their length in both directions, plus extra for overlaps. Bars to buy = total length ÷ the stock bar length.

Worked example

A 20 × 10 ft slab with bars 12 inches apart needs 10 bars running lengthwise and 20 running across, 385 linear feet. With 10 percent for overlaps that is 423.5 ft, or 22 bars that are 20 ft long.

Good to know

  • Where two bars meet end to end, overlap them at least 40 bar diameters: about 15 inches for #3 and 20 inches for #4.
  • Hold rebar up off the ground on chairs so it sits in the middle of the slab.
  • Number 3 (3/8 inch) bar suits patios and walkways. Number 4 (1/2 inch) is typical for driveways and footings.

Common questions

How far apart should rebar be in a concrete slab?

Twelve to 18 inches on center in both directions covers most residential slabs. Heavier loads call for closer spacing.

Do I need rebar in a 4 inch slab?

A patio or walkway can use wire mesh or fiber instead. Rebar is worth it for driveways, slabs on poor soil, and anything that carries a structure.

What size rebar should I use?

Number 3 bar for light slabs and number 4 for driveways and footings. An engineer or your building department sets the size for structural work.
