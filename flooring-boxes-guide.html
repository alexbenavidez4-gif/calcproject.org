How Many Boxes of Flooring Do You Actually Need?

← All Guides

The flooring calculator's math is simple — area divided by box coverage, plus waste. That's correct, but a few real-world details change what "correct" actually means once you're standing in the aisle deciding how many boxes to load into the cart.

Buy From the Same Run Number

Laminate, vinyl, and even hardwood are manufactured in batches — often called a "run" or "lot" number, printed somewhere on the box. Color and finish can vary slightly between runs, even under the same product name. If you buy your main order today and go back for one more box in three weeks, ask the store to check whether they still have your same run. A close-but-not-quite match is usually fine underneath furniture, but obvious in open floor space under good light.

Installation Pattern Changes Your Waste

Running planks straight down the room is the lowest-waste option — the standard 10% allowance covers it. Diagonal layouts push that closer to 15%, and herringbone or chevron patterns (increasingly popular, and strikingly more wasteful) often need 20% or more. Set the calculator's waste field to match your actual pattern, not the default.

Rule of thumb: order one full extra box beyond your waste-adjusted total, specifically set aside for repairs down the road — same logic as keeping spare tile.

Check the Box, Not a Guess

Box coverage varies a lot between products — commonly anywhere from 18 to 24 square feet for laminate, and it's printed right on the box. Plug in the real number for the exact product you're buying rather than leaving the calculator's default in place; a wrong assumption here throws off the whole estimate.
