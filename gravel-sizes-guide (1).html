Gravel Sizes Explained: Which One for Which Project

← All Guides

"Gravel" isn't a single product — it's a category, and the size and shape of the stone you pick changes whether a project holds up or shifts apart within a season.

Pea Gravel (about 3/8")

Small, smooth, and rounded — comfortable to walk on barefoot, which makes it a common choice for walkways, playground surfacing, and decorative garden beds. The rounded shape is also its weakness structurally: the stones roll against each other rather than locking together, so pea gravel isn't a good pick anywhere that needs to bear vehicle weight, like a driveway.

#57 Stone (about 3/4")

Angular, crushed stone — the standard material for driveways and drainage beds. Because the pieces are angular rather than rounded, they interlock and compact under weight instead of rolling apart, which is exactly what you want under a car or in a drainage trench that needs to stay put.

Crushed Stone / Road Base (mixed fines to about 1")

This includes stone dust and smaller particles that compact into a dense, hard-packed surface. It's rarely the visible top layer — it's typically used as a sub-base underneath pavers, larger decorative stone, or a driveway's top layer, giving everything above it a stable foundation.

Layered projects are normal: most driveways use road base compacted first, then a top layer of #57 stone on top. The calculator handles one layer at a time — run it twice, once per layer, for a project like this.
