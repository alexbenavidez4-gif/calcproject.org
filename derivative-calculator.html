How it works, examples and FAQ

The power rule says the derivative of c·x^n is c·n·x^(n−1). Multiply by the power, then lower the power by one. The derivative of a plain number is 0.

Example: f(x) = 3x^4 − 2x^2 + 5x − 7. Term by term: 3x^4 → 12x^3, −2x^2 → −4x, 5x → 5, −7 → 0. So f'(x) = 12x^3 − 4x + 5.

The derivative tells you the slope of the curve at any point. Evaluating at x = 2 gives the slope of the tangent line there.

Heads up: this version handles polynomials. Trig, exponential and product/quotient rules are on the roadmap.

Can it do sin, cos, e^x or ln?
Not yet. This first version covers polynomials, which is most of an intro calculus course.

How do I type x squared?
x^2. A term like 5x^3 can be typed 5x^3 or 5*x^3.

What does the derivative mean?
It is the instantaneous rate of change, the slope of the tangent line at each point.
